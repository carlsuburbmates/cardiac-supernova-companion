Side 1 • During Crisis

The Four Pillars

  • 1. Conscious Boundary Crossing Set down the armor. Lower your eyes to theirs. Stay anchored in yourself.
  • 2. The Silent Posture Silence the urge to fix or give advice. Match and steady your breathing.
  • 3. Holding Space for the Whole Truth Let their sorrow stand without sanitising it with cheerful clichés.
  • 4. Radical Accompaniment Go where they are without demanding an immediate happy ending.

Side 2 • After the Shift

The Second Question

When a difficult event ends, skip the automatic Australian evasion: "She'll be right, mate."

"That was brutal today. How are your ribs feeling?"

Hold the Rope: Listening to despair means watching for danger. If you hear acute risk or suicidality, never keep it secret. Protect their life immediately.

Chapter 14

The Five-Minute Decontamination Guide

Four physical threshold rituals to mark the line between what belongs to the world and what belongs to your home.

01

The Threshold of Water

At shift end, stand at the sink. Lather warm soap across your hands, wrists, and forearms. Watch the water drain and speak silently:

"I gave you my skill. I gave you my presence. The work is finished. Your sorrow belongs to the ward; it does not belong to my bed."
02

The Shedding of the Uniform

Never travel home wearing the clothes you wore in the crucible. Changing into soft, civilian clothes sends an unmistakable biological signal to your nervous system that the tactical deployment is over.

03

The Car Sanctuary

Take five minutes alone in your vehicle before turning the ignition key. No catastrophic news on the radio, no angry debrief calls. Rest your hands on the wheel, close your eyes, take three deep physiological sighs, and let your body arrive.

04

The Second Question

When a shift ends after a horrific event, do not settle for the automatic evasion. Look your colleague in the eye, step into the quiet, and ask: "That was brutal today. How are your ribs feeling?"
